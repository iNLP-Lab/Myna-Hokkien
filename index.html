Myna-Hokkien.

Myna-Hokkien is an open-source end-to-end conversational speech model for Singapore Hokkien. Users speak to the model in Hokkien and the model replies in natural-sounding Hokkien speech—no text round-trip required, though text input and output are also supported.

In brief

A native voice model for an underserved language.

Hokkien is the native language of tens of millions of speakers across Asia, yet it remains almost entirely absent from mainstream speech AI: no existing omni-style model—open or closed—natively supports Hokkien.

Myna-Hokkien is an attempt to close that gap, and to leave behind a reusable recipe for other low-resource language communities to do the same.

    02 · Possibilities

    What this approach could make possible

    An end-to-end Hokkien model opens three directions: richer speech interaction, useful Hokkien voice applications, and a foundation others can adapt.

    Current release note

    Myna-Hokkien is an early spoken question-answering model. Its Hokkien remains imperfect, its task coverage is narrow, and its speech does not yet carry much emotional variation.

    1. 01
      Affect-aware, emotionally expressive conversation

      An end-to-end speech model can learn more than the words being said. In principle, it can use paralinguistic cues—prosody, pace, loudness, timbre, and emotion—to understand how someone speaks and answer with a context-appropriate tone. Myna-Hokkien does not yet do this reliably; the architecture makes it possible, while expressive data determines whether it can actually be learned.

    2. 02
      General Hokkien voice applications

      Use the same conversational layer wherever speaking is easier or more natural than typing.

      • Family and elder-facing assistants for everyday help, explanations, reminders, and conversation.
      • Community and public-service access through kiosks, helplines, and service navigation after domain-specific safety work.
      • Language learning and oral heritage for pronunciation, sayings, and spoken practice without forcing every exchange through writing.
      • Local, hands-free help for drafting messages, brainstorming, and practical questions while working or moving.
    3. 03
      A reusable foundation for adaptation

      Developers can start from Myna-Hokkien instead of rebuilding Hokkien speech capability from zero: adapt it for a particular task, improve its Hokkien language or accent coverage, or add new speaking behaviours. The same training recipe can also help other low-resource language communities build their own native speech models.

    03 · Limitations

    It can answer. It cannot yet feel every answer.

    The model's words may be sympathetic, excited, or playful, but its voice still has limited emotional range. We do not yet have enough varied, expressive Hokkien speech data to teach that range reliably.

    • The current task coverage is narrow: mainly spoken question answering, not a general Hokkien agent.
    • Emotion, speaking style, and fine-grained delivery cannot be controlled reliably.
    • The ten clips above are selected listening examples, not a fixed public benchmark.
    • Responses can still be inaccurate, awkward, repetitive, or inconsistent; high-stakes use requires human oversight.
    • Accent and vocabulary coverage lean toward the data we have, and will not represent every Hokkien-speaking community equally.
    Have expressive Hokkien speech data?

    Emotion-rich conversation, varied speakers, and carefully aligned recordings would directly address one of the release's largest gaps.

    04 · Next

    Smaller to run. Broader in what it can do.

    The next releases will explore the same native speech-to-speech idea with smaller weights, and extend beyond today's spoken-QA focus.

    1. 01
      Release lighter checkpoints

      Make experimentation and deployment possible on more modest hardware.

    2. 02
      Cover more tasks

      Move beyond answering questions toward summarisation, guided workflows, learning, and other useful spoken interactions.

    3. 03
      Evaluate with the community

      Measure usefulness, naturalness, and failure modes with Hokkien speakers across ages and backgrounds.

    Why now

    Keep a spoken language useful in spoken interfaces.

    Singapore Hokkien carries family life, humour, memory, and everyday knowledge, yet it remains largely absent from modern voice systems. Myna-Hokkien is one attempt to make that gap audible—and to make the work reusable by other low-resource language communities.
